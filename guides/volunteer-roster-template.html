Volunteer Roster Template

A simple, copy-and-use structure for rostering volunteers across weeks or shifts — built for churches, sports clubs, community meals, school PTAs, and any other group that runs on a recurring roster.

Every volunteer roster — however it's built — is really just five pieces of information kept consistent from week to week: who, doing what, when, whether they've confirmed, and who covers if they can't make it. Miss one of those and the roster starts generating group texts instead of answering questions on its own.

Why "Backup" is its own column, not an afterthought: the roster that actually survives a Saturday-night cancellation is the one where every slot already has a next-best person written down — not one where the coordinator has to think of someone at 9pm.

Setting it up

  1. List every role you actually need filled — not every role that exists. A role nobody's rostered against in three months is noise on the sheet; retire it or fold it into another.
  2. Add one row per role per date, for as many weeks ahead as you comfortably plan (four to eight weeks is the usual sweet spot — far enough that people can plan around it, not so far that availability guesses go stale).
  3. Ask availability before you assign, not after. A short message — "which of the next 6 Sundays can you do?" — turns assigning into matching rather than guessing and re-asking.
  4. Fill Volunteer and Backup together. If you only ever fill the primary slot, every no-show becomes an emergency instead of a one-line lookup.
  5. Track a rolling count per person (a simple "times rostered this month" column works) so the same three reliable people don't quietly end up doing everything.
  6. Send reminders a few days out, not the morning of. By the time someone's getting ready for the day, a schedule change is a scramble rather than a heads-up.

Where a spreadsheet starts to strain

This structure works well by hand for small, steady teams. It tends to break down at a fairly predictable point: once you're juggling more than a handful of roles, tracking who prefers what, or fielding "I can't make it" messages faster than you can update the sheet and text the backup yourself.
