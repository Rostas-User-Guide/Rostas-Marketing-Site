Volunteer Reminder Messages: Text & Email Templates

The right message at the right time is most of what keeps a roster running. Here are seven ready-to-use wordings, from asking for availability to saying thank you. Copy them, change the names and dates, and send.

A few rules that make any message work

  • Lead with the date and the role. "Sunday 8 March, welcome desk" is what people need; everything else is extra.
  • Keep texts short. One or two sentences. Save the detail for email.
  • Make replying easy. Tell people exactly what to send back ("reply YES or NO"), so nobody has to work out what you want.
  • Send at sensible times. Not before 8am or after 9pm, unless it's genuinely urgent.

1. Asking for availability

Send this before you build the roster. Asking which dates people can't do gets shorter, more accurate answers.

Text or email

Hi [Name], I'm putting together the roster for [March and April]. Are there any of these dates you can't do? [1, 8, 15, 22, 29 March, 5, 12, 19, 26 April]. Just reply with the dates you're away. Thanks!

2. Sending out the roster

Email · Subject: The [team] roster for [March and April]

Hi everyone,

The roster for [March and April] is below (and on the noticeboard). Please check your dates now and put them in your calendar.

If you can't make one of your dates, let me know as early as you can, even weeks ahead. The earlier I know, the easier it is to find someone.

Thanks for all you do,
[Your name]

3. Reminder a few days before

Three days ahead is about right: close enough to be top of mind, early enough to swap if plans have changed.

Email · Subject: You're on [role] this [Sunday], [date]

Hi [Name], a quick reminder that you're on [role] this [Sunday, 8 March], starting [9:30am]. [Any details: where to find the keys, what to bring.]

Still good to go? Reply YES to confirm. If something's come up, reply and let me know, and I'll find someone else.

4. The day-before text

Text

Hi [Name], just a reminder you're on [role] tomorrow at [9:30am]. Thanks! Reply if you can't make it.

5. Asking someone to fill in

Ask one person at a time, not a group. A group text leaves five people wondering who's going to answer. (The no-show guide explains why.)

Text

Hi [Name], [Sam] can't do [role] on [Sunday 8 March] at [9:30am]. Could you fill in? Reply YES or NO. No problem at all if you can't.

6. Confirming the swap

Close the loop with both people, so nobody turns up twice or not at all.

Text, to the person filling in

Thanks [Name]! You're confirmed for [role] on [Sunday 8 March] at [9:30am].

Text, to the person who couldn't come

All sorted, [Name]: [Alex] is covering [role] on [Sunday]. Thanks for letting me know early.

7. Saying thank you

Specific beats general. Mention what they did and why it mattered.

Text or email

Hi [Name], thanks so much for stepping in on [sound desk] on Sunday at short notice. It made a real difference, and I really appreciate it.

Thank-yous are part of keeping people. Volunteers who feel noticed keep saying yes. There's more on that in the guide to preventing volunteer burnout.

Rostas asks for availability, emails each volunteer a few days before their date, sends a day-before reminder, and finds a fill-in one person at a time when someone can't make it. Volunteers answer with a tap in the Rostas app, or by text or email. It never texts during the quiet hours you choose.
