How to Set Up a Sports Club Volunteer Roster

Every club runs on volunteers: the canteen, the bar, lines and nets, the scorebook, washing the playing gear. When the roster works, Saturday runs itself. When it doesn't, the same handful of parents and committee members end up doing everything.

1. List the jobs for a home game

Start with a normal home fixture and write down every job, from opening up to locking the clubrooms. Common ones:

  • Ground set-up and pack-down: posts, nets, corner flags, line marking.
  • Canteen or barbecue, and the bar if you have one (check who holds the right licence).
  • Match-day officials: scorer, timekeeper, touch judges or umpires.
  • Gate or gear: taking entry fees, handing out and collecting bibs and balls.
  • Team jobs: oranges or half-time snacks, washing the uniforms, transport to away games.

Mark which jobs are needed every home game and which only on some days (a tournament, a prizegiving, a club day).

2. Decide who's on the list for each job

Some jobs suit anyone; others need a skill or a certificate. Make a list of who can do each one. For junior clubs, the pool is usually the parents, so think in families: count turns per family rather than per parent, so a family with two parents isn't asked twice as often as a family with one. Families with children in several teams may also prefer their turns on a day they're already at the ground.

3. Work from the draw, not the calendar

A sports roster follows the season draw. Roster only the days you're at home, and expect the draw to change: rain-outs, byes and rescheduled games are normal. Build the roster a few weeks at a time rather than the whole season in one go, so a change to the draw doesn't mean redoing everything.

Ask for availability before each block. School holidays, tournaments and family weekends away are easy to plan around if you know about them in advance, and much harder to fix on the Friday night.

4. Share it fairly, and show that it's fair

Nothing kills goodwill faster than a parent noticing they've done canteen three times while others haven't done it once. A few habits help:

  • Set a fair share up front. "Every family does two canteen turns a season" is clear and easy to accept.
  • Count every turn, including swaps and fill-ins, and keep the tally visible to the committee.
  • Spread the early and late jobs. The 7am set-up and the last pack-down shouldn't always fall to the same people.
  • Honour preferences. Someone who hates the barbecue but loves keeping score will turn up more reliably for the scorebook.

Our guide to preventing volunteer burnout has more on keeping the load even.

5. Remind people, and make swaps easy

Send a reminder a few days before and again the day before the game, with the time and the job. Make it easy to say "can't make it" early, so you can find someone else on Thursday rather than Saturday morning. The reminder templates and the no-show guide cover both.

Tell new families at registration. The best time to explain the roster is when a family joins, not halfway through the season. A short line on the registration form ("every family helps with two canteen turns and one set-up") sets the expectation from day one.

A fair roster without the spreadsheet

Rostas asks for availability, builds the roster within each person's limits ("max once a month") and preferences, and rosters people in the same household on the same date, so a family makes one trip to the ground. Volunteers answer in the app or by text or email, reminders go out before each game, and when someone can't make it Rostas asks the next-best person, one at a time.
